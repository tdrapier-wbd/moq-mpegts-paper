# Test 83: an SRT leg beside a MoQ or segmented-HTTP leg, merged or switched

**State: Parts A and B run on the MoQ and segmented legs; criteria 1, 2, 5, 6 and 8 hold, 4 and 7 are reported, and 3 holds except on TSDuck's segments with their added pair removed, where it fails as predicted.** An SRT leg merges with an opaque leg or the `seg` lane's slices without loss through every fault, inside an ST 2022-7 class only with SRT's latency matched; matched, a switch between SRT and the media-aware leg or the lane's slices costs the background's hole, and unmatched skips toward the leading leg, 2.3–2.5 s against a 1.2 s lag and 5.3–5.7 s against 5.3 s. Over 72 h of the live feed (Part C) the new-plane legs delivered what SRT delivered, unit for unit, outside three events: two hosts' unattended upgrades and the source's clock step, which cost the MoQ legs 6–12 s that SRT passed; the grader's negative control misnames one frame at a few blackouts (criterion 9 fails on the grader). Across a 117 ms path to another region (Part D, the grader fixed) 24 h of the shadow held the same outside the receiving host's upgrade, but for one video group the media-aware leg skipped, and Part B's matched cells held as on one host (criterion 11 holds); under bursts the relay gave up on a subscription's groups after a stall of about a quarter of its delay (criterion 12 fails), the export having stated half its delay; on upstream's fix the exports evicted only at stalls that reach their delay, but current `main`'s relay evicts again, at 3–4 s blackouts where `3c0daa21` does not, and from stalls of 0.56 s and 2.04 s with qlog on, each eviction losing 0.4–5.1 s of video. Part E waits on the IRDs of B-6.

## Objective

[Architecture](../docs/architecture.md) §5 measures redundant pairs within one data plane, and §11 offers a "transport-swappable hedge", a managed transport kept beside the new plane for the most critical routes, by reasoning only. An interim deployment would run the proven point-to-point leg (SRT, or AWS MediaConnect's) beside a MoQ or segmented-HTTP leg of the same source, and protect each with the other. No pair has mixed planes. This test asks:

- **Part A, merge:** do an SRT leg and an opaque MoQ leg of one source, each through its own stream-clocked groomer, emit the same RTP datagrams, and does an ST 2022-7 receiver merge them hitlessly through each leg's faults? The same for an SRT leg and a segmented leg, as packaged and with the packager's added tables removed.
- **Part B, switch:** across an SRT leg and a media-aware MoQ leg, and an SRT leg and a segmented leg, what does [T80](test-80-video-aware-receiver-switch.md)'s switch cost at a fault and at a commanded cut, with SRT's latency matched to the other leg's and not, and what changes in the tables at the cut?
- **Part C, shadow:** over 72 h of the live feed, does each new-plane leg deliver what the SRT leg delivers?
- **Part D, across a path:** Part B's matched cells and 24 h of Part C with every leg crossing a real path to a receiver in another region.
- **Part E:** an IRD's own input switch between the SRT and a new-plane send of [`srt-send.sh`](scripts/srt-send.sh), and a hardware ST 2022-7 merge of Part A's pair.

## Predictions, stated before running

- **A, opaque:** identical datagrams. Both legs carry the source's packets verbatim, continuity counters included ([T4](test-4-remote-e2e-srt.md), [T48](test-48-passthrough-export.md)), and the stream-clocked groomer takes each packet's slot from the stream, not from its arrival (Architecture §5.1), so the pair merges wherever the legs' delay difference fits the receiver's window.
- **A, segmented:** not mergeable as packaged, since `tsp -O hls` adds a PAT/PMT pair to each segment under its own counters ([Evidence](evidence.md) §3.1); identical once that pair is removed, if nothing else differs.
- **B:** with the legs arriving within 0.3 s, T80's result: no decoder hole beyond the stream's own and at most two frames repeated. Unmatched, a repeat or a hole of the lag, since T80's switch pairs by arrival. At each cut the decoder sees the source's tables give way to the export's, or the reverse, which a software decoder ignores.

**Part C.** On one receiving host, `srt-send.sh`'s `rx` lane as the reference beside `ma`, `pt` and `seg`, for 72 h. Fixed before the first graded run: the rig is [`t83c-run.sh`](scripts/t83c-run.sh), the grader [`t83c-shadow.py`](scripts/t83c-shadow.py). Each lane is called on loopback at 1,000 ms latency through TSDuck's TR 101 290 counters into the grader's parser, which keeps only each unit's key and digest, for 60 s; the first 120 s are not graded. A unit is an audio frame, a video or other PES, or an SCTE-35 section, keyed by PID and PTS (a section by its CRC). Three rules come from two 6 min pilots, each against the source as `rx` carries it:

- **Audio by frame.** The source's audio PES are fixed-size and not frame-aligned, while the export's carry whole frames, so frames are cut from each PID's stream.
- **PTS slack.** The export's timestamps sit one 90 kHz tick below the source's, so a unit joins one within two ticks.
- **Video digests.** The export re-emits H.264 parameter sets in its own order: on a keyframe its SPS and PPS come before the access unit delimiter, which H.264 puts first, and a PPS the source repeats inside the GOP is dropped. Delimiters, parameter sets and filler are therefore left out of a video unit's digest. Those differences, and the export's PAT, PMT and SDT intervals that TSDuck flags, are reported under criterion 10 as the export's, not counted as content.

The positive control is `rx` parsed a second time; the negative is `rx` through the grader's dropper, which removes one PES in 997 on each video and audio PID and a 2 s blackout every 30 min, and logs each unit it touches from the bytes it removed: removed, damaged, or swallowed (a frame whose header a damaged frame before it overruns). Criterion 9 holds when the copy matches throughout, and the cut lacks every removed or swallowed unit inside the graded window, lacks or alters every damaged one, and names none the dropper did not touch. In the second pilot, with one PES in 97 and a blackout every 90 s, it held exactly: 1,021 removed or swallowed, 25 damaged. **Predicted:** `pt` and `seg` match `rx` unit for unit, as they did in both pilots; `ma` matches on every unit under these rules; every difference falls in a lane's or the source's interruption.

## Pass criteria, fixed before running

1. **Valid (A).** Each control loses nothing. The oracle merges T12's same-plane opaque pair with no loss (positive control) and grades an arrival-clocked groomer pair unmergeable (negative control).
2. **Identity (A).** On the control, at least 99.99 % of the datagrams that share a sequence number are byte-identical, RTP header and payload, between the SRT and the opaque leg.
3. **Hitless (A).** In every fault cell the merged output has no lost datagram and no continuity error, with the legs' delay difference inside the oracle's window. The difference is reported with the ST 2022-7 class it fits.
4. **Segmented (A).** Identity reported as packaged and with the added pair removed; criterion 3 graded on the second.
5. **Valid (B).** Each control's decoder hole is the background's, and T80's same-plane `lookback` cell reproduces on the build under test.
6. **Matched (B).** At each fault and each commanded cut: no decoder hole beyond the background's, no corrupt picture, at most two frames repeated.
7. **Unmatched (B).** Reported, not graded: the hole or the repeat against the legs' lag.
8. **Tables (B).** Every table, version and PID that differs across each cut listed; no SCTE-35 section lost across a cut.
9. **Valid (C).** The grader names every unit removed from the negative control and none from the positive.
10. **Shadow (C).** Every content difference between a new-plane leg and `rx` attributed to one leg and a cause; none left unexplained.
11. **Across a path (D).** Criteria 6, 8 and 10 with every leg crossing the path, each difference attributed to a leg, the path or the source.
12. **The skip's mechanism (D).** Under bursts that black out the relay's flow to the receiving host, each media-aware export skips a group (an eviction logged, its video lacking) at every burst whose stall on its connection, from the burst's start to the first acknowledgement of data the relay sent after it, exceeds the export's delay by more than 0.5 s, and at none whose stall falls short of it by more than 0.5 s; neither skips in the control or outside a burst's window. Each skip is matched in the relay's qlog by a stream reset on that connection.
13. **Hardware (E).** Reported: the IRD's picture, sound, alarms and re-lock time at each cut, with the analyser's P1/P2 across it; the hardware merge against criterion 3.

## Limits, fixed in advance

- A software decoder and a reference merge until Part E; whether an IRD rides a cut whose tables change is Part E's alone.
- One clip, one host first; the cross-host repeat is one path.
- The SRT leg is TSDuck's throughout; a managed service's, such as MediaConnect's, may handle latency and loss differently, and is not measured.
- Within its plane the opaque pair leaves its importer single ([Architecture](../docs/architecture.md) §5.2); here the SRT leg, with its own ingest, covers that importer, so Part A cannot show what the importer's loss costs a same-plane pair.
